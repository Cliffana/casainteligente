Features to Consider Before Buying

Navigation: LiDAR vs Gyroscope vs Random

Navigation is the most important feature. defines whether the robot cleans efficiently or wanders around aimlessly.

LiDAR — uses a laser to map the home in real time. The robot knows where it is, where it has already cleaned and where it is going. Cleaning in organized queues. Best technology, present in Roborock, Xiaomi E10, Dreame.

Gyroscope — uses sensors to determine orientation. Less accurate than LiDAR but much better than random. Tapo RV10 uses this system.

Random — the robot walks until it hits a wall, changes direction, repeats. Inefficient, but cheap. Avoid these models.

Suction Power (Pa)

  • 2000-2500Pa: enough for dust and crumbs
  • 2500-3000Pa: ideal for pet hair and low carpets
  • 4000-5500Pa: for high carpets and deep cleaning

Mop

Most models up to €300 have a damp cloth mop — useful for daily maintenance but not a substitute for scrubbing with a bucket. Top models have a rotating mop that really scrubs.

Self-Cleaning Station

The top models (€600+) have stations that automatically empty dust for weeks. Practical but expensive. On mid-range models, the tank is emptied every 2-3 cleanings.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it worth buying a robot vacuum cleaner?
Yes, if you have a house with open spaces and few carpets. A robot vacuum cleaner effortlessly keeps your floors clean every day. For homes with a lot of furniture on the floor or multiple floors, it may be less practical.
How much does a robot vacuum cleaner cost?
Mid-range models (€150-300) vacuum and some even mop. The top ones (€600+) have smart mapping, self-cleaning stations and superior battery life. The best value is between €180-250.
Do I need Internet to use it?
Yes, most need WiFi for initial setup and smart features (programming, maps, voice control). Once configured, it works offline.
Does it work with Alexa and Google Home?
Most recent models work with both. You can say Alexa, tell the robot to vacuum the room, or Hey Google, start cleaning.
