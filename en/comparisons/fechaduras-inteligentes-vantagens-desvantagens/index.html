Smart Locks — Advantages, Disadvantages and Is It Worth It?

03/07/2026 5 min read

Complete analysis of the advantages and disadvantages of smart locks. Safety, convenience, installation, prices and risks — everything you need to know before you buy.

Table of Contents
Summary
Smart locks are one of the most polarizing devices in the smart home. Some people love convenience, others worry about security. This guide objectively looks at the pros and cons to help you decide if it makes sense for you.

Advantages of Smart Locks

1. Never Need Physical Keys Again

This is the biggest advantage. With a smart lock, you can enter your home with: - Numeric code (keyboard) - Digital printing - Mobile app (Bluetooth or WiFi) - RFID Card - Voice (Alexa/Google, although for security reasons many block this option)

Goodbye to lost, borrowed or forgotten keys at home.

2. Remote Access Control

Are you at work and your child is home from school? Release the door with a tap on your phone. Do you have a delivery scheduled? Give the courier a temporary code that expires after 30 minutes.

3. Temporary Codes

This is one of the most useful features. You can create codes that: - They expired after X hours/days - Only open on certain days of the week - They are valid once (ideal for cleaning or repairs)

4. Access History

You know exactly who came in and at what time. The app records each opening with identification (code used, fingerprint, app, etc.). Useful for confirming that the kids got home from school or that the cleaning service arrived on time.

5. Integration with Routines

The lock can trigger automations: "when you unlock the door → turn on the entrance light, turn off the alarm, adjust the temperature". Or "when lock at 10pm → turn everything off and turn on night mode".

6. Reversible Installation

Contrary to what many people think, installation does not require work. Most only replace the interior handle or cylinder. If you move house, uninstall it in 10 minutes and take it with you.

Disadvantages of Smart Locks

1. High Price

A quality smart lock costs between €80 and €250. Cheap brands (€30-50) exist on AliExpress, but their reliability and security are questionable.

2. Dependence on Batteries

All smart locks are battery operated (normally 4 AA batteries, lasting 6-12 months). Forgetting to change the batteries can leave you out of the house — even though there's always a physical emergency key.

3. Digital Security (Real Risks)

The risks exist but are often exaggerated. The realistic scenario is not a Russian hacker opening your door, but rather: - Shared code that has never been changed (ex-boyfriend, former employee) - Outdated firmware with known vulnerabilities - App with excessive permissions that can expose data

With a recognized brand and careful configuration, the smart lock is as safe as a quality mechanical lock.

4. Limited Compatibility

Not all locks fit all doors. Before purchasing, check: - Distance between holes (normally 54mm or 72mm) - Door thickness (minimum 35mm for most) - Cylinder type (European, Swedish, etc.)

5. High Entry Price for Useful Features

The features that really matter (temporary codes, history, integration) are only available on models starting at €120. The cheapest models are "smart" in name only — they connect to the app but don't have the features that justify the investment.

6. Not Everyone Has a Physical Emergency Key

Some models (especially the cheapest ones) do not have a physical key. If the electronics fail and the battery is empty, you could be locked out of your home. Always choose models with an emergency key.

When it’s worth it
- Have children who come home alone - Receives frequent deliveries - Do you have a maid or babysitter - Rent a house (AirBnB) - Want to monitor input/output - Already have other smart devices
When it's not worth it
- Lives alone and never loses keys - Has non-standard ports (difficult compatibility) - Limited budget (below €80) - You don't want to depend on batteries - Concerns about digital security

Final Verdict

Smart locks are not for everyone. For families with children, AirBnB owners, or anyone who receives a lot of visits/deliveries, the convenience justifies the price. For those who live alone and never lose keys, a quality mechanical lock makes more sense.

If you are going to buy: invest at least €120, choose a recognized brand (Nuki, Yale, Aqara, August), confirm compatibility with your door and never deactivate the physical emergency key.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are smart locks safe?
Yes, as long as you choose recognized brands (Yale, August, Aqara, Nuki) and keep the firmware updated. The main risk is not the lock being digitally broken, but rather wrong configurations (codes shared without control, authentication deactivation). A smart lock is only as secure as the configuration you make.
Do I need to change the door to install?
No. Most smart locks only replace the cylinder or interior handle, keeping the door and existing lock. The installation is reversible — if you change your mind, it goes back to normal in 10 minutes.
Does it work if you run out of Internet?
Yes, smart locks work offline. The code, physical key and fingerprint continue to work even without WiFi. Only the remote control (app outside the home) and notifications are unavailable.
What if the battery runs out?
All smart locks have a low battery warning (app and/or beep). If the battery dies completely, you can use an external 9V battery on the lock terminals to temporarily turn it on, or use the physical emergency key.
Is it worth buying a smart lock?
It depends on your lifestyle. It's really worth it if: you receive frequent visitors (deliveries, cleaning, babysitting), you have family members who regularly lose keys, or you want to monitor who comes and goes. If you live alone and never lose keys, it may not justify the investment.
