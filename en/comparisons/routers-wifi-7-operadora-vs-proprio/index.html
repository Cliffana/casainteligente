The Problem with ISP Routers

The routers that MEO, Vodafone, and Nos provide are functional for the basics: browsing the web, watching Netflix on a tablet. But they have serious limitations:

  • Weak CPU: low-cost processors that choke with many devices
  • Little RAM: typically 128-256MB, insufficient for 15+ devices
  • Limited WiFi: most are still WiFi 5 or basic WiFi 6, not WiFi 7
  • Locked firmware: you can't change advanced settings, DNS, VPN
  • Rare updates: ISPs update the firmware once or twice, then forget about it
  • Monthly rental: you're paying 2-5€/month for the router on your bill, which means 120-300€ over 5 years

Bottom line: you're paying an amount that could buy a much better router.

What Changes with WiFi 7

WiFi 7 (IEEE 802.11be) is the biggest evolution in wireless networking in recent years. Even on an affordable dual-band router, you can already feel the difference:

Feature WiFi 5 WiFi 6 WiFi 7
Max speed 3.5 Gbps 9.6 Gbps 46 Gbps
Channel width 160 MHz 160 MHz 320 MHz
Latency ~10ms ~5ms ~1ms
MLO No No Yes
4K QAM No 1024-QAM 4096-QAM

MLO (Multi-Link Operation) is the big new feature: the device can connect to both bands simultaneously (2.4GHz + 5GHz), increasing speed and stability. If one gets interference, the other keeps going.

How to Use Your Own Router with MEO, Vodafone, or Nos

Option 1: Bridge Mode (Recommended)

Connect the ISP router in bridge mode (it works only as a modem) and use your router to manage the network. This way you keep your landline and TV if needed.

  1. Connect the ethernet cable from the ISP router to the WAN port of your router
  2. Log into the ISP router and enable bridge mode (or DMZ)
  3. Configure your router normally

Option 2: Full Replacement (Internet Only)

If you only have internet (no landline), you can fully replace it:

  • MEO/Nos: configure with DHCP (automatic in most cases)
  • Vodafone: configure PPPoE with the credentials (username: vodafone@vodafone.pt, password: vodafone)
Pros of Buying Your Own Router
- Much faster and more stable - Future-proof WiFi 7 - Full control (VPN, DNS, QoS) - Save money in the long run - Can take it when switching ISPs
Cons
- Initial investment (one-time purchase) - Requires basic setup - Support is on your own - May lose ISP features (TV, phone)

Final Verdict

If you have internet for the basics (2-3 people, occasional streaming), the ISP router is enough. But if you want to get the most out of your fiber without spending much, an own WiFi 7 router is the best choice.

In the long run, you'll still save money — ISP router rental costs more than a WiFi 7 router in just 1-2 years.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it worth buying your own router instead of using the ISP's?
It depends. If you have more than 10 connected devices, stream 4K, game online, or work from home, yes. ISP routers are basic and choke with many devices. A 60-100€ router already offers better stability, range, and features.
Does WiFi 7 work in Portugal?
Yes, WiFi 7 (802.11be) works in Portugal. The frequencies are the same as WiFi 6 (2.4GHz, 5GHz). You need a WiFi 7 router and compatible devices. Most 2025+ smartphones and laptops already support WiFi 7.
What's the difference between WiFi 7 and WiFi 6?
WiFi 7 is much faster (up to 46 Gbps vs 9.6 Gbps for WiFi 6), has ultra-low latency (1ms vs 10ms), and supports 320 MHz bandwidth (vs 160 MHz). It also introduces MLO (Multi-Link Operation) which allows using multiple bands simultaneously for greater stability.
Do I need a triband router?
No, a dual-band WiFi 7 router is enough for most homes. Triband (with 6GHz band) makes sense if you have many high-end devices, but for family use with 10-20 devices, a dual-band WiFi 7 is plenty and much cheaper.
Do MEO or Vodafone let me use my own router?
Yes, all ISPs in Portugal allow you to use your own router. You can put the ISP router in bridge mode (it passes through the signal only, no routing) and use your router to manage the network. Or you can fully replace it if you know the PPPoE/VLAN settings.
